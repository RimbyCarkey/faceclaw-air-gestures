Faceclaw Privacy Policy

Faceclaw's own usage statistics are off unless you opt in. Separately, Apple collects and shares information when you use an iOS TestFlight build, even when Faceclaw statistics are set to None. Information you voluntarily send us, such as feedback, may also identify you. This policy describes these separate channels and the third-party services you can choose to use.

Usage Statistics

The first time you connect your glasses, Faceclaw asks whether to share usage statistics with its developers, to help them fix problems and improve the app. The choices are None, Minimal and Full, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy > Faceclaw statistics. Until you choose, it is None. This setting controls only Faceclaw's own statistics.

With None, Faceclaw sends and stores none of its own usage statistics. This does not disable Apple's TestFlight reports or services you choose to use.

With Minimal, Faceclaw keeps counts on your phone and sends them about once a day to stats.faceclaw.org, a server run by Faceclaw's developers:

  • - the Faceclaw version, whether it runs on Android or iOS, and whether it is
  • an official release or a build someone compiled themselves;
  • - whether glasses and a ring are paired, and which devices (glasses, ring,
  • Wear OS watch) were in use each day;
  • - counts of connection events: connection attempts, disconnections and
  • their causes, message timeouts and retries, Bluetooth error codes, and
  • how long connections lasted.

With Full, it also sends:

  • - which kinds of API key are set (for example, that an Anthropic key is
  • set), but never the keys themselves;
  • - how many terminal and T3 Code connections are set up, and which of
  • Faceclaw's built-in fonts is in use;
  • - the values of settings chosen from fixed options, such as on/off
  • switches and pick-one lists. Settings you type in, such as addresses,
  • URLs and names, are never sent.

Faceclaw's own statistics never include your name or contact details, your location, Bluetooth device addresses, anything you say or type, or what is shown on your glasses. Each report carries a random identifier, created when you turn statistics on, which links your reports to each other but not to you. Turning statistics off deletes that identifier and everything kept on the phone, and stops all reports; turning them back on starts a new identifier. The statistics server doesn't record the IP addresses reports come from.

iOS and TestFlight

For TestFlight builds, Apple automatically collects crash reports and beta usage information and shares them with us. This includes installations, sessions, crashes, app/build and OS versions, device model and identifier, and language. It is separate from Faceclaw statistics, with no in-app opt-out.

Joining only through a public TestFlight link hides your name and email from us, not from Apple. Email invitations can identify you to us. Apple also collects your device's name (not shared with us) and may use your IP address to estimate your region for beta availability.

Feedback is optional. Comments, screenshots and diagnostics submitted through TestFlight go to Apple and us. Public-link testers can omit their email in the feedback form. Email sent directly to us reveals your email even after public-link enrollment, but is not shared with Apple. Check screenshots and comments for private content; feedback can include device state such as battery level, free storage and network type.

Faceclaw has no public iOS API to switch off TestFlight's automatic reporting. Its statistics setting and iOS tracking permissions do not control it. To leave, select Stop Testing on Faceclaw's TestFlight information page, then stop using and uninstall the beta. This does not erase previous reports. Self-built installations do not use TestFlight reporting.

For ordinary iOS analytics, use iPhone Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, including Share With App Developers. Faceclaw cannot change these system settings. TestFlight crash reporting applies regardless of those settings. See https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/app-analytics/.

We use TestFlight reports only to improve Faceclaw, not for advertising or sharing with third parties. Apple retains feedback for one year and may keep crash/usage reports until bugs are resolved. For access/deletion requests, contact us using the developer email in TestFlight; manage Apple's data at https://privacy.apple.com/.

Apple's details: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/test-flight/ and https://testflight.apple.com/.

Audio

Faceclaw allows users to transcribe audio (for use with the voice assistant, the Transcribe app, typing into Terminal, and other apps). The default setting is that this is done using an on-device model running on your phone. If an on-device model is selected, audio never leaves your device, and is not stored after it is processed.

Users can also provide their own API key for a third-party transcription service such as OpenAI Whisper, ElevenLabs, or Soniox, which may offer better accuracy. Faceclaw never transmits audio to any party other than a user-selected transcription service. If a third-party transcription service is selected, audio will be sent to that third party, is linked to the account with which that API key was created, and is subject to that party's privacy and retention policies:

  • OpenAI: https://openai.com/policies/row-privacy-policy/
  • ElevenLabs: https://elevenlabs.io/privacy-policy
  • Soniox: https://soniox.com/policies/privacy-policy

Third party data retention and privacy policies may differ based on the API key provided. API keys are part of a relationship between the user and the API provider; Faceclaw does not manage accounts or process billing information.

AI Assistant Transcripts

Faceclaw has a builtin AI voice assistant. Using a cloud model requires providing an API key for a model provider such as Anthropic, OpenAI, or Venice, or configuring an OpenAI-compatible endpoint such as a private Open WebUI server. Prompts and responses are subject to the selected provider or server operator's privacy policy:

  • OpenAI: https://openai.com/policies/row-privacy-policy/
  • Anthropic: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy
  • Venice: https://venice.ai/privacy

With an OpenAI-compatible provider, the configured server receives the assistant system prompt, conversation history, available tool definitions, and tool results needed to answer. Its URL, model ID, and optional API key are stored on the phone. Faceclaw does not operate that server and cannot make representations about its logging or retention; for a private Open WebUI server, the server's operator controls those policies. If you choose Discover models, Faceclaw also requests that server's model catalog using the same configured URL and optional API key.

If you enable Venice web search for the official Venice API URL, supported models may generate web-search queries from your assistant conversation. Those queries and their results are processed by Venice's search service and are subject to Venice's privacy policy and any applicable charges. This setting is off by default and is never sent to Open WebUI or other compatible providers.

AI conversation histories and memory files may be stored on your phone. Faceclaw does not transmit voice-assistant prompts or responses to any party other than a user-selected AI model provider. The voice assistant is given tools with which it is able to access most things on the user's phone, including all content displayed on the glasses, location, notifications, and content accessible through the Terminal app.

The AI assistant can take actions on the user's behalf, including sharing any information it has access to. The user is responsible for any instructions they give it.

Location

Faceclaw has a builtin Navigate app, which provides turn-by-turn directions. Using the Navigate app requires providing an API key for Mapbox. While the Navigate app is active, the user's exact location and selected destination are transmitted to Mapbox, and are subject to Mapbox's privacy policy.

  • Mapbox: https://www.mapbox.com/legal/privacy

The Weather app will retrieve the user's coarse location, and will use it to query a third-party weather information provider: the US National Weather Service, then Open-Meteo if the location is outside the US. Outside the US, it also asks the phone's built-in geocoder (Google's on most Android phones, Apple's on iOS) for the name of the place.

  • Open-Meteo: https://open-meteo.com/en/terms

Spotify

On iOS, the Music app, Glanceboard and the voice assistant can show and control Spotify through Spotify's Web API, once the user has created their own Spotify developer app and signed in. Signing in happens on Spotify's website in the system's sign-in sheet; Faceclaw never sees the password, and stores the resulting sign-in tokens on the phone. While Spotify is shown or used, Faceclaw requests the user's playback state, queue, library and search results from Spotify and sends it playback commands, directly from the phone. This is subject to Spotify's privacy policy.

  • Spotify: https://www.spotify.com/legal/privacy-policy/

The EvenHub app store is subject to Even Realities' privacy policy: https://support.evenrealities.com/hc/en-us/articles/14127464826511-Privacy-Policy. Apps downloaded from the EvenHub app store come with their own privacy policies.

Neural Band and Air Gestures integration (this fork)

The optional native Neural Band integration uses Air Gestures for band connection, profiles, phone controls and handwriting. Band gestures, motion/handwriting events, profile associations and drafts are processed between your band and phone. The integration does not upload them to this fork's developers, GitHub or GitLab. Delivering a draft to another app or assistant can send it to the providers configured for that destination, as described elsewhere in this policy.

Meta sign-in, ownership and firmware services

Enrollment through Meta is optional: you may instead import a compatible owner bundle you already hold. When you choose Meta sign-in, Meta receives the information you enter on its sign-in pages and the normal connection/browser information. The sign-in uses its own WebView cookie store, separate from Faceclaw's other WebViews. The resulting Meta account session is retained on the phone, encrypted with an Android Keystore key; private owner keys and account sessions are excluded from Android backups. An exported owner bundle authorizes band control and must be protected by you.

Claiming a band sends the signed-in account session token, band serial number, device certificates, an app public key, nonces and ownership receipt/signature data to Meta's hardware service at graph.facebook-hardware.com. This happens as part of the enrollment flow, after the user starts and confirms claiming. Claiming can change the band's owner and unlink its previous controller. This integration is not an official Meta service, and does not assume that a user-provided API key has already established consent: the account sign-in and ownership confirmation are separate user choices.

The optional Meta firmware tools can send the account session token, band serial, firmware/version information and update-session identifiers to Meta's firmware services, and download packages from the URLs Meta supplies. Downloading and installing firmware are separate from updating this Android app. Importing an owner bundle or using local band navigation does not require giving this fork's developers your Meta credentials or keys.

Meta controls its services and their retention practices. Its policies are at: https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy/ https://www.meta.com/legal/privacy-policy/

Optional Android access and phone features

Air Gestures' accessibility features require Android's explicit accessibility access. Depending on the feature enabled, they can inspect the foreground app and editable field, enter recognized text, or perform navigation/touch actions. Notification access exposes notification content for the selected notification features. Phone-call permissions support call-related actions. These permissions are not required merely to use Faceclaw's on-phone preview.

Speech and assistant actions can pass text or control to Android's configured speech engine or assistant app; those apps' own processing and policies apply. If a Google service is selected, see https://policies.google.com/privacy. Air Gestures automation broadcasts can be received by other installed automation apps. Its existing profile-announcement receiver can accept profile context from other local apps. Native glasses input itself uses an in-process band listener, not an exported automation receiver or the watch/TCP input interface.

Unified app updates and development automation

When the embedded update checker runs, it requests release information from api.github.com for this fork and stores release/version information locally. The request does not contain your Meta account token, owner key, band serial, gesture history or draft text. GitHub receives normal request metadata such as the connecting IP address and User-Agent. Following an update link opens GitHub's release/download pages, which are subject to GitHub's policies: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement

The repository's source-update automation runs on GitHub Actions, not on your phone. It checks public GitHub and GitLab release feeds, fetches public source, and prepares review branches and build artifacts. It does not read your phone's app storage, band keys or Meta session. GitLab's policy is at: https://about.gitlab.com/privacy/

Issues, pull requests, screenshots and logs voluntarily posted to this public repository are public. Do not include account tokens, owner bundles, private keys, personal messages or unredacted device/account identifiers in reports.

Wear OS Watch

The optional Faceclaw watch app (wear/) talks to the phone over Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer, the same channel the watch uses for notifications and other companion apps. Gestures, commands, the watch's own speech-recognition results, and the mirrored glasses status and assistant replies travel between your watch and your phone over their Bluetooth (or, when paired that way, Wi-Fi) link; Faceclaw does not send any of it to a server. Speech recognized on the watch uses the watch's system speech recognizer, which may send audio to Google under Google's privacy policy:

  • Google: https://policies.google.com/privacy

The phone does nothing watch-related on phones without Google Play services.
